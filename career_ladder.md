# Career-Ladder Template (one page per function)

**Why this exists:** Employees often say they don't know what it takes to get promoted, and that outcomes depend on who their manager is. A written ladder gives managers and employees the same yardstick.

**How to use it:** Copy this page for each function (Engineering, Operations, Sales, ...). Fill the bracketed parts with the function's real expectations. Review with managers before sharing with employees.

---

## Function: [e.g. Operations / Collections]

| Level | Scope of impact | What "good" looks like | Evidence a manager can point to | Typical time at level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 [Associate] | Own tasks | Delivers assigned work reliably; asks for help early | [e.g. SLA adherence, quality audits] | [12-18 mo] |
| L2 [Senior Associate] | Own process | Improves how their own work runs; supports newer teammates | [e.g. process fix shipped, onboarding buddy feedback] | [18-24 mo] |
| L3 [Lead] | Team outcomes | Owns a team metric; coaches others; handles escalations | [e.g. metric improvement over 2 quarters] | [24-36 mo] |
| L4 [Manager] | Multiple teams | Builds capability; makes trade-offs; develops future leads | [e.g. team retention, promotions from the team] | [n/a] |

## Rules that make it fair
1. **Same evidence for everyone.** Promotion cases use the "evidence" column, not impressions.
2. **No surprises.** Managers hold a career conversation with each report at least twice a year, and write down the gap to the next level.
3. **Calibration.** Managers in the same function review promotion cases together, so one manager's standard doesn't differ from another's.
4. **Visible timelines.** Employees can see typical time at level and the next review date.
5. **Lateral growth counts.** When promotion slots are limited, offer scope growth (new project, mentoring, rotation) with a clear route back to level.
