# Making Manager Quality and Career Progression Visible
*A two-page proposal for Razorpay People Operations, by Annwesha*

## The observation
Anonymous employee feedback about fast-growing fintechs often says two things: the experience depends heavily on the manager, and the path to promotion isn't clear. Both are fixable by People Operations, and both are measurable.

## Why it matters
When a team's manager is weaker, people tend to be less engaged, wait longer for promotion, and leave sooner. Replacing an employee is expensive in recruiting time, lost context and team morale. If a small group of managers drives a large share of exits, a targeted coaching program costs far less than company-wide retention spend.

## What I built (a private demo)
1. **Dashboard.** Shows engagement, promotion wait and attrition by manager and function, and lists teams to support first. The demo uses synthetic data, but you can drop in a CSV from your own HRIS and it redraws instantly. The file is processed inside your browser, so nothing is uploaded and I never see it. It also works without an engagement score.
2. **Career-ladder template.** One page per function with levels, evidence and fair-process rules.
3. **8-question pulse survey** with scoring and an action playbook.

## How implementing this helps Razorpay
- **Spend effort where it pays.** Instead of company-wide retention programs, People Ops sees the handful of teams where exits and stalled promotions cluster, and coaches those managers first.
- **Make growth transparent.** A written ladder per function means employees know what the next level takes, and promotion decisions across managers can be compared fairly. This also reduces the "depends who your manager is" perception.
- **Catch problems early.** A quarterly pulse plus HRIS data flags falling engagement before resignations arrive, when a conversation can still change the outcome.
- **Put a number on it.** The dashboard's estimator turns an exit-rate gap into an avoided-exit and cost range, so leadership can see the case for investing in managers in their own terms.
- **Low cost to start.** It runs on data People Ops already has (HRIS export plus one short survey) and needs no new software to pilot.

## How it could run at Razorpay
- **Week 1-2:** Pilot with one function. Draft ladders with 3-4 managers. Run the pulse survey.
- **Week 3-4:** Load survey and HRIS data into the dashboard (on your side, no data shared). Identify the 5-8 teams to support first.
- **Month 2-3:** Manager coaching and career conversations. Re-pulse at 90 days.

## What to measure
Engagement by team, 12-month attrition, average months since promotion, and the share of employees who say they know the next-level criteria. Success looks like a narrower gap between the strongest and weakest teams.

## What I don't know, and the limits
- This is built from public signals, not Razorpay's data. Your internal numbers may show a different priority, and I'd value hearing what's actually true.
- The dashboard shows associations, not proof of cause. A weak team can also reflect a hard market or a recent reorg, so findings need conversations before action.
- The value estimator uses assumptions (replacement cost, share of the gap closed) that you would replace with your own figures.
